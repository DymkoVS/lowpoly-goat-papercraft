# Building the goat

A short version of this guide is on pages 2–3 of every PDF.

## Materials and tools

- **Paper and board.** Tested: plain 80 g/m² office paper glued onto thin cardboard. The small goat (v1) can also be printed directly on 160 g/m² card. The large goat (v2) needs cardboard.
- **Knife.** Craft knife or snap-off utility knife, metal ruler, cutting mat. Scissors for rough trimming.
- **Tweezers** — straight and bent: to press tabs in tight places (horns, hooves, the last centimetres of the belly).
- **Scoring tool.** Wooden stick, empty ballpoint pen or ball embossing stylus.
- **Glue.** Fast glue: superglue gel or contact cement. Apply with a toothpick. Keep nail polish remover handy for your fingers.

## Printing

1. Paper size **A4**. Many drivers default to Letter — check it.
2. Scale **100 %** ("Actual size", not "Fit to page").
3. Measure the **50 mm** bar in the sheet header with a ruler.
4. For heavy paper choose the "Cardstock / Heavy paper" media type. Stay within your printer's maximum paper weight.

## Gluing onto cardboard

- Sheet **face up**: lines and numbers must stay visible. Mountain and valley folds are marked for the printed side being the outside of the goat.
- Use a **dry** glue: glue stick, spray adhesive, double-sided film. Wet PVA makes the paper wavy.
- Press under a stack of books for an hour, then cut through both layers at once.

## Legend

| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Solid | cut |
| Dash-dot (— · —) | mountain fold: away from you, the ridge points at you |
| Dashed (- - -) | valley fold: towards you, a groove |
| Grey trapezoid | glue tab, folded inwards along its base |
| Number at an edge | edge ID: edges with the same number are glued together |
| Large label "7L" | piece number; L — goat's left side, R — right side |

Nearly flat edges (less than 2°) have no line and are not folded.

## Steps

1. **Score before cutting** — every dashed and dash-dot line, including tab bases, along a ruler.
2. **Cut** along solid lines.
3. **Fold**: mountains away from you, valleys towards you, tabs inwards.
4. **Assemble** (tested order):
   1. the whole head — horns, ears, muzzle;
   2. front legs;
   3. hind legs;
   4. sides — from the hind legs towards the front legs. You get two halves (L and R) with legs;
   5. join the halves along the top midline: back, neck, chest;
   6. attach the head;
   7. close the belly last (see below).

## Closing the figure

On the belly seam **all tabs are on the left (L) pieces**. The right half lies over the protruding tabs, nothing has to be held from inside. The edge numbers of this seam are printed on page 3 of the PDF.

- Close **from both ends towards the middle**: near the front legs, near the hind legs, the middle of the belly last.
- Do the last 5–10 cm **the contact way**: a thin layer of glue on the tabs and on the mating edge, wait 5–10 minutes, press. It grabs instantly.
- Before closing you can put a weight into the legs (sand, coins). Glue 2–3 cross bulkheads into a large goat.

The belly is underneath, between the legs, so even an imperfect last seam is barely visible.

## Tips

- Glue in small steps: 2–3 numbers at a time.
- If an edge number ended up on a narrow face, write it on the back with a pencil right after cutting.
- Score double layers (paper + cardboard) harder and strictly along the ruler.
